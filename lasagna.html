Lasagna

Origin of Lasagna Dish

Lasagna is a classic Italian dish believed to have originated in the region of Emilia-Romagna, Italy. Its name comes from the Greek word "Laganon", one of the earliest forms of pasta made from layers of dough. The modern version of lasagna as we know it--layered sheets of pasta with rich ragu (meat sauce), creamy bechamel, and cheese, especially Parmigiano-Reggiano--developed in Italy during the Middle Ages. Traditionally baked until bubbly and golden, lasagna is now a beloved comfort food around the world, with countless variations including vegetarian, seafood, and even dessert versions. It's known for its hearty, layered goodness and bold Italian flavors.

Ingredients

  • Lasagna noodles (pre-cooked or oven-ready)
  • Ground beef or Italian sausage
  • Onion
  • Tomato sauce or marinara sauce
  • Tomato paste
  • Olive oil
  • Ricotta cheese
  • Mozzarella cheese (shredded)
  • Parmesan cheese (grated)
  • Egg (to mix with ricotta)
  • Fresh or dried basil and oregano
  • Salt and black pepper

Steps

  1. Cook the meat: In a pan, saute chopped onions and garlic in olive oil, then add ground beef or sausage and cook until browned.
  2. Make the sauce: Add tomato sauce, tomato paste, herbs, salt, and pepper to the meat. Simmer for 15-20 minutes.
  3. Mix the ricotta filling: In a bowl, combine ricotta cheese, egg, and a bit of salt and pepper.
  4. Preheat the oven: Set to 180 degrees celsius (350 degrees Fahrenheit)
  5. Assemble the layers: In a baking dish, layer sauce, noodles, ricotta mixture, and shredded mozarella. Repeat layers and top with Parmesan.
  6. Bake: Cover with foil and bake for 25-30 minutes. Uncover the bake and extra 10 minutes until cheese is golden.
  7. Cool and serve: Let it rest for 10 minutes before slicing and serving.
